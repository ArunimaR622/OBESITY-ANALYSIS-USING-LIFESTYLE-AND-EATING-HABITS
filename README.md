# OBESITY-ANALYSIS-USING-LIFESTYLE-AND-EATING-HABITS
A Data-Driven Study on Lifestyle Factors Influencing Obesity
Project Overview

This project explores how daily habits — such as diet, physical activity, sleep, transportation, and high-calorie food consumption — contribute to obesity levels.
Using a synthetic dataset, we perform descriptive and exploratory analysis to uncover relationships between lifestyle patterns and health risk indicators.

The project includes data preprocessing, feature engineering, univariate, bivariate & multivariate analysis, and visualizations.

Dataset Description

Rows: 2887

Columns: 29

Contains demographic, behavioral, and lifestyle variables:

Age, Gender, Height, Weight

Eating habits: CH2O, FCVC, FAVC

Physical activity (FAF)

Screen time (TUE)

Smoking & alcohol habits

Transportation mode

High-calorie food flags

Engineered scores:

Lifestyle Score

Diet Quality Score

Activity Score

Health Risk Score

BMI & BMI Category

📈 Key Findings

Higher physical activity strongly correlates with lower health risk.

High-calorie food consumption increases health risk score significantly.

Obesity Levels II & III show the lowest activity frequency.

Transport modes like walking/biking contribute to better activity scores.

Diet quality and lifestyle scores are strong predictors of BMI categories.

📌 Conclusion

The project successfully demonstrates how lifestyle factors influence obesity.
Healthy behaviors — such as regular physical activity, low-calorie food intake, and active transportation — significantly reduce health risks. The engineered scores provide a comprehensive view of an individual’s overall wellness profile.
